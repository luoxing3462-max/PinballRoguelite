# Gameplay Module

Primary entries: GameplayScreen.Setup, GameplayScreen.Fire, GameplayScreen.FinishLaunchAfterDelay, PinballBoard.Build, PinballBoard.CreateScoringBins, Marble.OnCollisionEnter2D, Marble.Settle, ScoreManager.AddBumperScore, ScoringZone.ResolveMultiplier, and CombatDisplay.PlayAttack.

GameplayScreen owns one level's marble selection, charge input, shot count, failure recovery, score settlement, and result transition. `LaunchPhysicsSettings` converts held time through its configurable AnimationCurve into a bounded launch impulse. GameplayScreen uses that impulse for both player release and enemy automatic firing. It instantiates GameplayUI.prefab through the registry in UI.prefab, while GameplayUiView only presents state and forwards button/hold input. CombatDisplay owns the Godot-style top combat lane inside the bound BattleInfo placeholders: the player stays fixed on the left, the enemy starts on the right, one launch settlement becomes one visible attack, and a surviving enemy advances by shot index.

`MarbleCatalog` owns the six playable marble definitions: normal, split, gravity, pierce, bounce, and lucky. A split marble requests its single split on leaving the launch route and entering the central playfield; GameplayScreen replaces it with two non-splitting child marbles that share the original launch settlement. A run equips exactly two selected IDs before map entry. GameplayScreen only accepts those two IDs for player input; enemy firing remains normal and independent. `ScoreManager` converts each accepted collision into a weighted contribution while retaining the shared eight-collision cap and base-damage cap, so child marbles and bounce chains cannot multiply a launch beyond the normal balance boundary.

PinballBoard owns physical board construction, the fixed lower-right launcher, independent rising lane, top turn, scoring row, and active-marble registry. `Build` derives the closed left, top, and right boundary walls plus the open bottom settlement region from `PlayfieldBounds`; `LauncherEntryGate` is a right-edge `PlatformEffector2D` that admits lane-to-board travel but blocks return into the launcher. Build selects one of four clearance-safe stagger templates when no seed is supplied. The default 16 bumpers are a 4×4 grid whose rows alternate by half a horizontal step; both spacing axes are calculated from the physical playfield bounds, and the complete grid has rotational symmetry so edge coverage alternates left/right without a vertical drop lane. Randomness chooses a template and swaps special bumpers only at existing grid points. `BumperLayoutSettings` exposes valid even counts, spacing scales, and minimum clearance; explicit seeds exist for deterministic verification.

The bottom settlement row always has eight shuffled slots: two blue ×1 slots preserve raw damage, one red ×0 slot discards it, and five purple ×? slots roll an inclusive ×2～×5 multiplier when entered. Seven narrow dividers leave enough width for the marble. ScoringZone resolves the rule, Marble preserves zero, and ScoreManager applies the resolved multiplier.

Preserve real Rigidbody2D travel from LaunchOrigin; launch routing must not teleport position or velocity. Marble clears stale motion once, applies the selected initial impulse with `ForceMode2D.Impulse`, and then receives no corrective movement in the playfield. Each configured marble uses continuous collision detection, a bounded maximum linear speed, and material bounciness capped at the gameplay-safe value so high-bounce shots cannot tunnel through boundary walls or accumulate unbounded energy. A prolonged near-stationary marble settles rather than receiving a hidden push. Template pegs must stay clear of one another, the side launch channel, the top entry route, and the bottom settlement row. Each Marble emits one damage event for each unique bumper it hits: repeated contact with the same bumper does not add damage, while a different bumper continues the score chain. ScoreManager accumulates the accepted collision events. Local collision behavior stays in Marble, Bumper2D, ScoringZone, and ScoreManager. Difficulty and score targets come from LevelConfig.
